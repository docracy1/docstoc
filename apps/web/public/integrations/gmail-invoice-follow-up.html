The problem

The invoice is 30 days overdue. The client hasn't replied to the first reminder, if you even sent one. You know you need to follow up from your own Gmail — clients trust an email from you, not an automated system — but starting from a blank compose window is where most follow-ups die.

The manual way

Open Gmail, hit compose, and write the follow-up from scratch — trying to sound firmer than the last email without sounding annoyed. Then remember to check for a reply.

The docstoc way

Connect Gmail once in your docstoc workspace. docstoc drafts a tone-matched follow-up — firmer as it gets more overdue — and saves it directly into your Gmail Drafts folder. It can also read client replies to suggest what to say next. You always review and hit send yourself; docstoc never sends on your behalf.

What lands in your Drafts folder

The draft is created as a normal Gmail draft. Open it like any other, read it, change the greeting, attach the invoice PDF and send it when you are ready. If you decide not to chase after all, delete it.

Using replies to decide the next step

Chasing often stalls after the first reply. A client may write that payment is coming on a given date, or that the invoice needs a purchase order number. docstoc can read client replies to suggest what to say next, which is a paid feature. You read the suggestion, adjust it and send it yourself.

  • Note any promised date and check the day after
  • Reply in the same thread so the history stays together
  • Send the missing reference or document straight away if the client asks for one

Why clients answer you and not a system

A message from your own address, in your usual voice, reads as personal. That is why docstoc stops at the draft and leaves sending to you. The Gmail draft feature needs a paid plan.

FAQ

Does docstoc send the email from my Gmail?

No. It saves a draft in Gmail. You review it and press send.

Can I use Gmail without connecting it?

Yes. Copy the draft from docstoc and paste it into a new Gmail message.

Is the Gmail connection free?

No. The Gmail draft feature requires a paid account.
