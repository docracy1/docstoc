Living Will (Advance Healthcare Directive)

I, [Your Full Legal Name], being of sound mind, make this declaration of my wishes regarding medical treatment if I become unable to communicate them myself.

1. Statement of Wishes

If I am diagnosed with a terminal condition or am permanently unconscious, and my doctors agree there is no reasonable hope of recovery, I direct that:

[ ] I want life-sustaining treatment withheld or withdrawn, allowing natural death, except for treatment to keep me comfortable.
[ ] I want life-sustaining treatment provided regardless of my condition.
[ ] Other instructions: [Describe]

2. Specific Treatments

Artificial nutrition/hydration: [Wishes]
CPR: [Wishes]
Mechanical ventilation: [Wishes]

3. Healthcare Agent (if also naming one)

I appoint [Name] as my healthcare agent to make medical decisions on my behalf if I cannot, consistent with the wishes stated above. [Note: a separate Healthcare Power of Attorney document may be required in your state for this appointment to be legally effective.]

4. Organ Donation

[ ] I wish to donate my organs/tissues upon death.
[ ] I do not wish to donate.

Signature: ______________________ Date: ____________

Witnesses (most states require 2 witnesses, and some require notarization):
Witness 1: ______________________ Date: ____________
Witness 2: ______________________ Date: ____________

This document is provided for informational and educational purposes only and is NOT a substitute for legal advice. Living wills have strict, state-specific execution requirements, and many states use their own official statutory form — check your state's specific form (often available free from your state health department or hospital) rather than relying solely on a generic template for something this important.

What a living will does

A living will records which medical treatment you want, or do not want, if you can no longer speak for yourself, for example in a terminal condition or permanent unconsciousness. It speaks for you when your doctors and family would otherwise have to guess.

It is different from a healthcare power of attorney, which names a person to make decisions for you. Many people sign both, so there is a written wish and a person to apply it.

Before you sign

  • Rules differ by country and by US state. Many states have their own form and require witnesses, a notary, or both. Check what your state or country requires before relying on this template.
  • Talk to your doctor about what treatments like CPR, ventilation or artificial nutrition mean in practice.
  • Tell the people close to you that the document exists and where it is.
  • Give copies to your doctor and your healthcare agent, and keep the original where it can be found quickly.

Keep it current

Review it after a major diagnosis, a move to another state or country, or a change in your views. A newer signed version replaces the old one, so destroy outdated copies.

FAQ

Is a free living will template legally valid?

It can be, if it is signed and witnessed the way your state or country requires. The template gives you the wording; the formal requirements decide whether it is binding.

Do I need a lawyer for a living will?

Not always. Many people complete a living will without one, but if your situation is complex or your family disagrees, legal advice is worth it.

What is the difference between a living will and a last will?

A living will covers medical decisions while you are alive. A last will covers what happens to your property after death.
