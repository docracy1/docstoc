How to fill in a payment request

A payment request asks someone inside or outside your company to release money for a specific purpose. It gets approved fast when the approver can answer three questions without writing back: what is it for, how much exactly, and where is the proof.

  • Requested by and date: the person who will answer questions about the request.
  • Payable to: the exact legal name on the invoice or bank account, not a nickname.
  • Amount and reason: one amount per request and one line on what it pays for.
  • Related invoice or PO number: lets accounts payable match the payment to the right record.
  • Supporting documentation: attach the invoice, receipt or contract before you send it.
  • Payment method: tick one, and add bank details only through your company's approved channel.

A short example

Requested by Maria Lopez on 3 March. Payable to Bright Signs LLC. Amount 1,240.00 for the trade fair banner, invoice 2025-118, PO 4471 attached. Payment by bank transfer. Approved by the marketing lead. That request needs no follow-up question, which is why it gets paid first.

Common reasons a request gets stuck

  • The amount on the request does not match the attached invoice.
  • The payee name differs from the name on the bank account.
  • No approver is named, or the approver is the same person who requested it.
  • Changed bank details arrive by email: confirm them by phone before anyone pays.

FAQ

What is the difference between a payment request and an invoice?

An invoice is what a supplier sends you to ask for payment. A payment request is your internal form asking your company to pay it, or a polite request to a customer when no invoice was issued.

Who should approve a payment request?

Someone other than the requester. A second person checking amount and payee is the simplest protection against errors and fraud.

Can I use this as a request for payment to a customer?

Yes, for a one-off amount. For recurring work, a proper invoice with your tax details is usually required.
