SSL For Free or docstoc: a ZeroSSL front end or Let's Encrypt in a workspace

SSL For Free hands out ZeroSSL certificates in a web form: docstoc issues Let's Encrypt certificates and keeps them with your business paperwork.

SSL product See pricing

What SSL For Free is

SSL For Free is a web service powered by ZeroSSL. It issues certificates through ACME (free 90-day certificates) or a web app and REST API, with email, HTTP and DNS verification. Its page says the private key is generated and encrypted on your device and downloaded only through your logged-in browser. It lists a free plan with 3 certificates and paid plans from $13.99 a month.

Where SSL For Free is stronger

  • It offers email, HTTP and DNS verification, so you can validate without touching DNS. docstoc uses DNS only.
  • The key is generated and encrypted on your device per the page, so the service does not hold a usable copy of it.
  • Paid plans list unlimited 90-day certificates and 1-year certificates. docstoc has a 5-slot cap and 90-day certificates only.
  • It names ACME access on paid plans for fully automated renewal with clients such as Certbot.

Where docstoc fits

  • Free covers 5 certificates, against 3 on SSL For Free's listed free plan at the time of writing.
  • You publish one DNS TXT record and verify. No file upload and no email alias.
  • Certificates, expiry reminders, invoices, templates and file certificates share one login.
  • Pro at $14.99 a month and Business at $39.99 cover the whole workspace, not only SSL.

Which one to choose

Choose SSL For Free if you want a quick web form, HTTP or email validation, annual certificates, or a larger number of wildcards, and you are happy with ZeroSSL behind it.

Choose docstoc if you manage a few hostnames, prefer DNS validation, and want certificates in the same account as invoice chasing and documents, with a reminder-led renewal.

FAQ

Is SSL For Free the same as ZeroSSL?

Closely related. SSL For Free's page says it is powered by ZeroSSL, which provides the infrastructure, and paid plans are sign-ups on ZeroSSL's app. docstoc is separate and uses Let's Encrypt.

Which gives more free certificates?

docstoc Free includes 5 certificates of 90 days; SSL For Free lists 3 on its free plan at the time of writing. Check both pages, as plans change.

Do I have to renew a docstoc certificate by hand?

Yes. You get a reminder about 30 days before expiry, click Renew, publish a new TXT value and verify. SSL For Free recommends automating renewal with ZeroSSL ACME or Certbot.
